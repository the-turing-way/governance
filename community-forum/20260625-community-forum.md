### Breakout rooms
Which activities do communities undertake that align strategic delivery and inclusion and belonging?
Which activities lead to conflicts between strategic delivery and inclusion and belonging?

#### Room 1

- Community forums like these where members can learn about updates and be a part of some of the decisions.
- "Get involved" pages. Gentle on-ramps. This way people can start by contributing something small, and slowly building up from there.
- Create spaces to bring target demographics closer to the project, so you'll make better decisions around representing their needs

Conflict: 
    - where the people running the inclusion spaces are a different set of people to those who actually have power within the project.
    - when people don't have enough time to help onboard new contributors

#### Room 3
Hastily planned activities often wind up being accidentally exclusionary - no time to plan, or to share context means smaller group gets involved 
Detailed documentation is necessary but not sufficient for inclusion - can feel quite isolating to be given documentation and left to "get on with it" 
International acknowledgements - timezone awareness, collaboration where we can, shifting times to help make it easier for others to join 

#### Room 4
Projects need stakeholder involvement
Meeting bringing in stakeholders, letting them express their needs are a good place to focus on belonging
Bringing in "strategic" voices who may not be representative of your community/users
Are inclusion and strategy really in conflict?
Diversity/inclusion is a positive thing, your strategy should be to encourage it
Time!
It can take more effort to make sure events/products are inclusive
If you have a deadline, it may be easier to skip considering inclusion
Related to time, what are your (organisational) priorities
If the measure of success is completing a task or making a product, it may be (more) acceptable to overlook inclusion
For example, not writing alt-text to save time
Meeting times to suite a majority which excludes community members
Open source can move slow
Finding consensus is difficult
